Read it, ask us anything, and let's go save some lives.

1 PURPOSE AND SCOPE
The simple version: we pull at-risk cats and kittens from euthanasia lists, shelters, and the streets, and you give them a safe place to heal, grow, and get socialized until they are ready for their forever homes. Some get adopted locally, some travel to partner rescues. This Agreement lays out who does what, and we log each placement in our Rescue Management System, or RMS, described in Section 7.
2 OWNERSHIP OF FOSTER ANIMALS
Every Foster Animal belongs to Pawsitive Transformations and stays ours the whole time it is with you. That is protection, not mistrust: it keeps the Rescue legally responsible for these cats, paying their vet bills, and able to step in for them. Fostering does not transfer ownership, no matter how long or how much you fall in love. See Section 9 for the plot twist where fosters adopt.

* Please do not sell, give away, trade, transfer, abandon, rehome, loan, or hand off any Foster Animal to anyone else, unless we ask you to in writing. If someone wants to adopt, point them to our adoption application (Section 9).
* Please do not promise a cat to anyone or tell people the cat is yours to place. Send all adoption interest our way through the standard application process, and we will take it from there.
* We may ask for a Foster Animal back at any time, for any reason or none. If we do, please return the cat along with any Rescue-provided supplies, medications, and records within the window we give you. This is rare and usually logistical, not a judgment on your care.
* If you are ever unable or unwilling to keep caring for a Foster Animal, for any reason, bring the cat back to us and no one else. Every one of these animals always has a home with the Rescue. This is the promise the clause protects, not a leash on you.
* The one hard line: refusing to return a Foster Animal on request is legally treated as unlawful conversion of the Rescue's property, and we can pursue the civil and criminal remedies available to us, including recovery of the animal, damages, and attorney's fees and costs.
3 TERM AND TERMINATION
This Agreement starts the day you sign and runs until either of us ends it, by a note via email or RMS message. The one catch: if you are wrapping up, it is not final until every Foster Animal in your care is safely back with us or moved wherever we direct. No cat gets left in limbo.
We may also pause or end placements and reclaim animals if we believe a cat's health, safety, or welfare is at risk, or the terms here are not honored. If that comes up, we would rather talk it through than surprise you.
4 STANDARD OF CARE
This is the heart of it: keeping these cats healthy and safe. Most is what any great cat person already does, plus a few rescue-specific must-dos. California law (Penal Code Section 597, animal cruelty and neglect) sets the floor; our care plans set the rest:

* Keep Foster Animals indoors, always. No outdoor roaming, balconies, patios, garages, or leashed strolls, unless we approve a secure enclosure like a catio in writing first.
* Fresh water always, the right food for their age and stage per the care plan, clean litter, and a safe, temperature-controlled space.
* Quarantine new arrivals from your own pets and other fosters for the period in the care plan (usually 10 to 14 days), in a separate room with their own supplies. This is the single best thing you can do to stop panleukopenia, upper respiratory infections, ringworm, and parasites from spreading. Boring, unglamorous, saves lives.
* Cat-proof the space: secure window screens, mind the doors so nobody bolts, and clear hazards like toxic plants, dangling cords, recliners (genuine kitten death traps), and unsecured windows.
* Keep your own animals current on core vaccines, and tell us if any resident pet gets sick during a placement.
* Follow the feeding, medication, socialization, and weight-check instructions in each care plan, including bottle-feeding schedules for the tiny ones.
* Never hit, physically punish, declaw, or allow any surgical or cosmetic alteration of a Foster Animal.
* Introductions between Foster Animals and your resident pets must be discussed with and approved by the Rescue in advance, and done per our introduction protocol. Because those introductions happen in your home and outside our supervision, the Rescue is not responsible for negative outcomes (injury, illness, or worse, to any animal) that result from improper handling or not following the agreed protocol.
Housekeeping on your end: fostering should not put you over your local pet-limit rules, and if you rent, make sure your landlord is on board. That part is on you, and we are happy to help you think it through.
5 SUPPLIES AND EXPENSES
Good news: we have got the supplies covered. We provide the daily necessities your Foster Animals need, including food (and kitten formula when there are bottle babies), litter, and basic gear like carriers, playpens, scales, heating pads, and bedding, as available. Your job is the love and labor; the shopping list is on us.

* The equipment we lend you stays ours; please return it in reasonable shape when the placement or this Agreement wraps up. Normal kitten wear and tear is expected.
* Use supplies as directed, and give us a reasonable heads-up when you are running low, not at midnight with a hungry neonate.
* Please stick to the diet in the care plan; check with us before switching a cat's food. Tiny tummies are dramatic.
* If you choose to buy extras on your own, that is generous but on you, and not reimbursable unless we approved it in writing first. Anything we did approve and you documented can be reimbursed or, if you prefer, counted as a donation.
* We do not cover everyday household costs (utilities, laundry, cleaning supplies, mileage) unless agreed in writing in advance.
6 VETERINARY AND MEDICAL CARE
Here is a weight off your shoulders: all the medical stuff is ours to handle and pay for. That means vaccinations, rabies shots when they are old enough, parasite treatment and prevention, FeLV/FIV testing per protocol, spay or neuter, microchipping, prescribed meds, and care for any illness or injury. You are never on the hook for a vet bill.

* Because we coordinate and cover the care, it must run through our designated vets and clinics. Please do not take a cat to your own vet, greenlight a treatment, or give any medication or supplement not in the care plan without checking with us first.
* Going off-script on vet care means those costs are not reimbursable, and unauthorized treatment can end a placement. When in doubt, call us.
* Give medications exactly as the care plan spells out, and log each dose in the RMS (Section 7).
* Emergencies: if a cat has a life-threatening emergency, call our emergency line right away at 714-721-8838. If you truly cannot reach us and it cannot wait, get the cat to our designated emergency clinic, Corona Emergency Animal Hospital, and reach us the moment you can. Euthanasia and treatment decisions are always ours to make, never something you carry alone.
* Tell us within 1 hour if you spot signs of illness or injury (lethargy, not eating, vomiting, diarrhea, sneezing, eye or nose gunk, weight loss or failure to gain, skin lesions), and right away for anything urgent. You know your foster cats best; loop us in early.
* Help us get cats to their scheduled vet visits, booster shots, and spay/neuter dates, and follow the before-and-after surgery instructions. Section 8 covers transport.
7 COMMUNICATION, RECORDS, AND REQUIRED RMS USE
Good information keeps cats alive and gets them adopted faster. Our Rescue Management System (RMS) is where it all lives. Here is what we count on from you:

* Use the RMS, our custom-built platform for foster and medical records, adoption applications, and marketing our cats to the public, for all record-keeping below. Using it is part of fostering with us, and we will get you set up and trained.
* You will get your own personal RMS login. Keep it safe, do not share it or let anyone else use your account, and tell us right away if it is lost, stolen, or compromised. Anything entered under your login is credited to you, which is why we keep it personal.
* Training and required reading: before a cat comes home with you, please finish the training and reading we assign in the RMS, plus any extra module for special placements like neonates, bottle babies, or medical cases. Here is the important part: when you mark an assigned item complete in the RMS, that click is your written confirmation that you read it, understood it, and agree to follow the protocols in it. The RMS keeps a timestamped record, and each protocol you acknowledge becomes part of this Agreement. If we later update and reassign a protocol, the same rule applies.
* Log weights in the RMS as the care plan directs (daily for neonates and bottle babies, at least weekly for everyone else), along with medication doses, bottle-feeding notes, and health observations. These numbers help our vets catch trouble early.
* Send regular updates on how each cat is doing (health, personality, socialization) at the frequency in the care plan, and reply to us within 24 hours, faster if a cat's health is involved.
* Share current, good photos (and video when we ask) of each cat at least weekly and on request, for medical check-ins and the adoption listings that find them homes.
* Give us a heads-up before any travel, moves, or household changes that could affect a placement, and check with us about care while you are away. Do not leave Foster Animals with a third party without running it by us first.
* Let us know promptly if your address, phone, or email changes.
8 ADOPTION EVENTS AND TRANSPORT
A cat nobody sees is a cat nobody adopts. Getting your fosters out into the world is a huge part of finding their people:

* Help get your Foster Animals to and from vet visits, spay/neuter dates, adoption events, and other Rescue happenings, always in a secure carrier.
* Come to adoption events with your fosters when scheduled. You do not have to make every one, but showing up is the general expectation. If you cannot attend or transport, tell us as early as you can, and no later than 48 hours out except for real emergencies, so we can line up a backup.
* The only real problem is repeated no-shows with no word, since a cat may be counting on that event. If it keeps happening we may reassign fosters or end the arrangement.
Using your own car for Rescue activities is your choice and appreciated. Keep your license and auto insurance current; your auto insurance is primary for anything involving your vehicle.
9 ADOPTIONS AND FOSTER ADOPTION ("FOSTER FAIL")
Final adoption decisions are ours: the who, when, and whether for each cat. That is not us waving away your voice. Nobody knows a foster cat like the person up at 3 a.m. with them, and your read genuinely shapes where they land. But fosters guide rather than formally approve, deny, veto, or hold a cat back. It keeps things fair for every cat and adopter.
Now, the best plot twist in rescue: the foster fail. If you fall for one of your fosters and want to make it official, we are thrilled, apply away. Fostering does not skip you to the front, though: you complete our standard adoption application and process, sign the adoption agreement, and pay the standard adoption fee unless we reduce or waive it in writing. Approval is not automatic, but a foster who has been rocking it has a lot going for them.

* The real perk: as the current foster, you get priority consideration over other similarly-qualified applicants, as long as you apply before we accept someone else's approved application for that cat. We cannot undo a finalized adoption, so tell us early; timing is everything.
* Until the adoption paperwork is fully signed by us, the cat is still officially ours and this Agreement still applies. After that, congratulations, you are a proud foster failure, the best kind.
* While a cat is still looking for a home, talk them up honestly and send interested folks to our application. Just do not promise, reserve, or informally hand off a cat, even to your lovely neighbor; everyone goes through the same door.
10 BITES, SCRATCHES, ESCAPE, AND DEATH
Nobody likes thinking about these, but a quick plan beats a panic. If any of the following happens, the golden rule is the same: tell us fast, and we handle the rest together. You are never alone in one of these moments.

* Bites: if a cat bites and breaks human skin, let us know within 24 hours.
* Escape or a missing cat: if a foster cat gets out or goes missing, call us right away and we will jump into recovery mode together. Speed is everything here, and no judgment; even seasoned fosters have had a door dash on them.
* If a cat passes away: unfortunately, losses happen. It is essential that you report losses immediately so the Rescue can advise on disposal of remains and any protocols or actions needed to protect other fosters and resident animals in your care.
* Injuries to people or animals: give us a prompt heads-up if a foster cat injures a person or another animal, so we can take action to implement any protocols or behavioral interventions that may be needed.
11 HOME ENVIRONMENT AND INSPECTIONS
The details you shared in your foster application (your home, the people in it, your own pets) should be accurate and kept current, so we can match you with cats that fit your setup. Everyone living with you should be on board with fostering, and you are the point person for how your household and guests treat the Foster Animals.
From time to time we may do a friendly home visit, virtual or in person, at a reasonable time and with reasonable notice, to see the cats thriving and lend a hand. The only firm part: turning away a reasonable welfare check means we will need to bring the cats back, because that visit is one of the few ways we can vouch for them.
Kids and cats are a wonderful combo with a grown-up in the mix: minors can interact with Foster Animals under adult supervision, and the primary caregiver on record needs to be 18 or older.
12 VOLUNTEER STATUS
You are a volunteer, a genuinely appreciated one. You are not an employee, contractor, agent, or legal representative of the Rescue, and this Agreement does not create a job, partnership, or agency relationship. There are no wages or benefits for fostering (and none expected), which also means no workers' comp or unemployment. Unless we authorize it in writing, please do not sign the Rescue up for obligations, speak to the media on our behalf, or fundraise in our name.
13 ASSUMPTION OF RISK
Real talk, because we would rather you know than be surprised. Rescue work is deeply rewarding and carries real risks, and we want you going in with eyes open. Those risks include bites and scratches, contagious illnesses, allergic reactions, property damage, illness or injury to your own pets despite good quarantine, and the emotional toll when an animal is sick or does not make it. Many of our cats arrive with unknown medical and behavioral histories, so surprises happen. This is why the quarantine protocols exist, to best protect you, your pets, and loved ones. By fostering, you take on these risks for yourself and your household knowingly and willingly.
14 RELEASE OF LIABILITY AND WAIVER
The formal legal language, with the plain-English version first so it is not a mystery: by fostering, you agree not to hold the Rescue and its people responsible if something goes wrong in the ordinary course of foster work, unless we were seriously careless or acted wrongfully on purpose. It is standard for volunteer and animal work and protects the organization so we can keep saving cats. The official wording:
To the fullest extent permitted by law, you, on behalf of yourself, your household members, heirs, and assigns, release, waive, and discharge Pawsitive Transformations and its directors, officers, employees, volunteers, and agents (the "Released Parties") from any and all claims, demands, damages, losses, and causes of action of every kind, whether known or unknown, arising out of or related to your foster activities, Foster Animals, or this Agreement, including claims for personal injury, illness, death, property damage, and injury to or death of your resident animals, except to the extent caused by the gross negligence or willful misconduct of a Released Party.
You expressly waive the protections of California Civil Code Section 1542, which provides: "A general release does not extend to claims that the creditor or releasing party does not know or suspect to exist in his or her favor at the time of executing the release and that, if known by him or her, would have materially affected his or her settlement with the debtor or released party."
15 INDEMNIFICATION
Companion to the last section, plain English first: if a problem is caused by you not following the Agreement or our instructions, or by your own negligence or intentional wrongdoing, that one is on you rather than the Rescue. Follow the care plan and communicate, and this clause stays theoretical. The formal wording:
You agree to indemnify, defend, and hold harmless the Released Parties from and against any third-party claims, damages, liabilities, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorney's fees) arising out of (a) your breach of this Agreement, (b) your negligence or willful misconduct, or (c) injury or damage caused by a Foster Animal while in your care to the extent attributable to your failure to follow this Agreement or the Rescue's instructions.
16 MEDIA RELEASE AND CONFIDENTIALITY
Two quick things about photos and privacy:

* The photos, videos, and updates you share of your fosters are gold for getting them adopted, so you give us permission to use them (and any pictures of you from Rescue events) for adoption promotion, fundraising, social media, and other good-cause purposes, at no cost. Camera-shy? Tell us in writing, and we will keep the spotlight on the cats.
* You will see behind-the-scenes info, including the RMS, adopter and applicant details, donor information, and internal records. Keep it confidential, during and after your fostering, and use the RMS only for Rescue purposes.
